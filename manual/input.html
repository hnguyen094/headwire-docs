Touch, pointer and keyboard

Everything you do on the stream goes to your Mac: your fingers, Apple Pencil, a trackpad or mouse, and a keyboard. The settings for each are in Settings › Touch and pointer and Settings › Keyboard.

Touch

In Settings › Touch and pointer, Touch picks how your fingers move the Mac's pointer.

A short video of this is on its way.
  • Point and tap: tap anything on the Mac's picture to click it, and drag with one finger to drag it, as on a touch screen.
  • Trackpad: the screen works like a Mac trackpad. Drag a finger to move the pointer, and tap to click wherever it is. Trackpad inertia keeps the pointer moving after a quick swipe, slowing to a stop.

In either mode:

ToDo this
Right-clickTap with two fingers, or touch and hold.
ScrollDrag two fingers. A quick flick keeps scrolling, as on a Mac.
ZoomPinch with two fingers.
Open Mission ControlSwipe up with three fingers.
Open App ExposéSwipe down with three fingers.
Move between SpacesSwipe left or right with three fingers.
Open AppsPinch with three or four fingers.
Show the desktopSpread three or four fingers.

The opposite gesture closes what one opened: swipe down to leave Mission Control, for example.

Pointer speed sets how far the pointer moves for your finger's travel. Reverse scrolling flips the direction Headwire scrolls, on top of your Mac's own setting.

On iPad, touch works while Headwire fills the screen. In a window, use Apple Pencil, a mouse or a trackpad.

Three-finger drag

With Three-finger drag on, drag with three fingers to move windows and select text, as with the Mac trackpad setting of the same name. Mission Control and Spaces then take four fingers. With Headwire Connect, the switch follows your Mac's own setting, and says it's From Headwire Connect on your Mac.

Apple Pencil

On iPad, Apple Pencil points straight at the Mac's picture, in full screen or in a window: touch to click, and draw to drag. Hovering moves the pointer without clicking.

A short video of this is on its way.

With Scribble on, under Apple Pencil in Settings › Touch and pointer, write in a text field on your Mac to type. Your handwriting turns into text as it does anywhere on iPad, in the languages you chose for Scribble. Scribble needs Headwire Connect, which tells Headwire where the Mac's text fields are.

A trackpad or mouse

A trackpad or mouse connected to this iPad moves the Mac's pointer. Click, scroll with two fingers or the wheel, and use a mouse's back and forward buttons as you would on the Mac.

A short video of this is on its way.
  • Pointer style draws the Mac's pointer as the Mac does, Mac, or as this iPad draws its own, iPad, over Headwire Connect's picture.
  • Snap to controls holds the pointer on buttons on your Mac, so clicks land on them, as the iPad's own pointer does. Where one control sits inside another, such as a close button on a tab, the pointer holds on the one inside, so you can reach it. In a browser tab, whose close button shows only under the pointer, the pointer moves freely until the button appears.
  • A clickable area too large to highlight, such as a wide row, gets a soft tint while the pointer is over it, as iPadOS tints one, and the pointer keeps its shape.

A keyboard

A keyboard connected to this iPad or iPhone types on your Mac, Command, Option and Control included. iPadOS keeps a few shortcuts, such as Command-Tab and Command-Space, for itself; keyboard shortcuts send them another way.

A short video of this is on its way.
  • Headwire keeps Control-Option-Command shortcuts for itself: with S, R and T for screenshots, recording and Live Text, and with a number to pick a Mac.
  • Over Bluetooth, your Mac reads keys by where they sit, so Headwire types through your Mac's keyboard layout: set it in Settings › Keyboard › Mac keyboard layout. Macs with Headwire Connect use their own layout.
  • If a key seems stuck on your Mac, tap Release all keys in Settings › Keyboard.

The on-screen keyboard

Tap Keyboard over the stream, or the keyboard key at the end of the key row, to bring up the on-screen keyboard and type on your Mac. Headwire moves the picture up, or fits it above the keyboard, so you can see what you type. Autocorrection in Settings › Keyboard turns the keyboard's corrections on or off.

Over Bluetooth, Headwire can only type characters your Mac's keyboard layout has. When it leaves some out, such as an emoji, a note over the stream names them. Headwire Connect types any character.

The Mac keys

The on-screen keyboard has no Command, Escape or arrow keys, so a strip of Mac keys rides on top of it.

A short video of this is on its way.
  • Command, Option, Control and Shift: tap one to hold it for the next key. Tap it twice to lock it for every key, and tap it again to let go.
  • Mac keys: Escape, Tab, the arrows, Forward delete, App Switcher, Spotlight and Paste to Mac. The App Switcher stays open until you tap Command.
  • Function keys: F1 to F12.
  • Your keys: keys of your own.
  • Keys for the app in front on your Mac, with Headwire Connect.

In Settings › Keyboard, Mac keys picks when the strip shows: Automatic shows it on iPhone, and on an iPad with no keyboard connected; or choose Show or Hide.

Custom keys

Make keys of your own for the Mac keys: a shortcut, some text, or a sequence of both. They show on a page of their own in the strip.

A short video of this is on its way.
  1. Open Settings › Keyboard › Custom keys, and tap Add key.
  2. Under Sends, choose Shortcut, Text or Sequence. For a sequence, build its steps with Add shortcut to sequence, Add text and Add pause.
  3. Under Show, choose whether the key shows a Title or a Symbol.
  4. Tap Save.
  • Keys are for the Mac you're using. Turn on Same keys on every Mac to share them across every Mac.
  • Keys for each app show while that app is in front on your Mac, with Headwire Connect, which tells Headwire which app it is. The list offers to add keys for the app in front now.
  • Text is typed through your Mac's keyboard layout.
  • On the strip's page of your keys, Add keys or Edit keys opens Custom keys.

Keyboard shortcuts

Some Mac shortcuts never reach Headwire, because iPadOS keeps them. Keyboard shortcuts send them from a different chord on your keyboard.

A short video of this is on its way.

Open Settings › Keyboard › Shortcuts. Headwire starts with these:

PressMac gets
Option-TabCommand-Tab. Keep Option held and press Tab again to move through the apps; let go to switch.
Option-SpaceCommand-Space, for Spotlight.
Control-Command-SpaceCommand-Space, for Spotlight.
  1. Tap Add Shortcut.
  2. Tap Record and press the chord on your keyboard. It needs Control, Option or Command, so the key still types on its own.
  3. Under Send, choose Keystroke and record what your Mac gets, or choose an action: Mission Control, App Exposé, Previous Space, Next Space or Show Desktop.
  4. Tap Save.

Delete a shortcut by swiping it. Restore Default Shortcuts brings back the ones Headwire started with.
