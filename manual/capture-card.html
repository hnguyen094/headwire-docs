Capture card and Bluetooth

The first way to use Headwire needs nothing installed on your Mac. A capture card brings the Mac’s screen and sound to your iPad, and Bluetooth lets your iPad be the Mac’s keyboard and mouse.

What you need

An iPad with a USB-C port, and a USB video capture card: a standard “HDMI to USB” capture card that takes HDMI in and plugs into USB-C. Your Mac sees the capture card as a display, so this works with a Mac mini that has no monitor, and it shows the login window too.

A short video of this is on its way.
  • The capture card plugs into your iPad, and an HDMI cable runs from your Mac to the capture card.
  • Bluetooth, on both your Mac and your iPad, for the keyboard and mouse.
  • Nothing to install on your Mac.

On iPhone, capture cards may not work, and Headwire says so in What works now. Headwire Connect brings your Mac’s screen to this iPhone by cable or wirelessly: see Headwire Connect.

The picture and sound

Plug the capture card into your iPad and open Headwire. The system treats a capture card as a camera and its sound as a microphone, so Headwire asks for both.

A short video of this is on its way.
  1. Connect an HDMI port on your Mac, or its USB-C or Thunderbolt port through an HDMI adapter, to the capture card.
  2. Plug the capture card into your iPad’s USB-C port.
  3. Open Headwire. When asked, allow Camera for the picture and Microphone for the sound.
  4. Your Mac’s screen fills this iPad. Headwire picks the capture card’s best format on its own.

Headwire only takes the capture card’s sound, never your iPad’s own microphone. If the sound runs ahead of or behind the picture, change Sound timing in Settings › Sound.

If you turned Camera off, Headwire shows Camera access is off with an Open Settings button. In the Settings app, open Apps › Headwire and turn on Camera, and Microphone for the sound.

To choose a resolution or frame rate yourself, see the capture card’s page in Settings. Headwire keeps what it learns about each capture card in its profile.

Your iPad as the Mac’s keyboard and mouse

Over Bluetooth, your Mac sees this iPad as a keyboard and mouse. You pair them once from your Mac, and from then on they connect by themselves.

A short video of this is on its way.
  1. Open Headwire and allow Bluetooth when asked.
  2. On your Mac, open System Settings › Bluetooth, find Headwire under Nearby Devices and click Connect.
  3. Both screens show a code. Check they match, then confirm on your Mac and tap Pair in the alert on this iPad.

Once paired, your Mac lists this iPad under its own name, not as Headwire. Touch, a trackpad, a mouse and a keyboard on this iPad now reach the Mac: see Touch, pointer and keyboard.

While a Mac has no way to control it yet, Settings lists the capture card’s Mac as Capture card. Its page has Control this Mac, with Connect over Bluetooth…, which gets this iPad ready for your Mac to connect, and Stop waiting while it waits. Once connected, Disconnect ends it.

Choosing which device gets input

Headwire types and points only on the device you choose. When a Bluetooth device starts using this iPad as its keyboard and nobody has chosen it yet, Headwire asks once: the alert reads A Bluetooth device connected, or names the device. Tap Use It to control it, or Not Now to leave it be.

Headwire doesn’t ask about a device that says it is an iPhone, iPad or Apple Watch, and doesn’t ask while a Headwire Connect is paired. To choose later, open Settings › Add Mac… › Connect over Bluetooth…, which lists the devices under Devices; tap one to choose it.

Pairing Bluetooth through Headwire Connect

If Headwire Connect is also on your Mac and connected, it can answer the Mac’s side of the Bluetooth pairing, so you compare one code on this iPad and never open the Mac’s Bluetooth settings.

A short video of this is on its way.
  1. In Headwire, open Settings › Add Mac… and, under Other ways, tap Connect over Bluetooth….
  2. Tap Pair. Headwire Connect asks your Mac to pair with this iPad.
  3. This iPad shows its own pairing alert. Check that the code in Headwire matches the one in the alert, then tap They match, and tap Pair in the alert.
  4. When it’s done, Headwire shows Paired.

If the codes differ, tap They don't match. Nothing is confirmed without your tap, and Headwire Connect gives up after a minute without one. If pairing doesn’t finish, Headwire says why and offers Try again; Troubleshooting has every message.
