Capture card and Bluetooth

Set up the way in that needs nothing installed on your Mac: a capture card brings the Mac’s screen and sound to your iPad, and Bluetooth makes your iPad the Mac’s keyboard and mouse.

What you need

A short video of this is on its way.
  • An iPad with a USB-C port.
  • A USB video capture card, the standard “HDMI to USB” kind.
  • An HDMI cable from your Mac to the capture card, or an HDMI adapter on your Mac’s USB-C or Thunderbolt port.
  • Bluetooth on your Mac and your iPad.

Your Mac sees the capture card as a display, so this works with a Mac mini that has no monitor, and it shows the login window too.

On iPhone, capture cards may not work, and Headwire says so in What works now. To see your Mac on this iPhone, use Headwire Connect, by cable or wirelessly.

See your Mac’s screen and hear its sound

A short video of this is on its way.
  1. Connect your Mac’s HDMI port to the capture card.
  2. Plug the capture card into your iPad’s USB-C port.
  3. Open Headwire.
  4. When Headwire asks, allow Camera for the picture. The system treats a capture card as a camera.
  5. When Headwire asks, allow Microphone for the sound. Your Mac’s screen fills this iPad.

Headwire picks the capture card’s best format by itself, and only takes the capture card’s sound, never your iPad’s own microphone.

  • To choose a resolution or frame rate yourself, turn on Advanced mode in Settings › General, then open Settings › Capture card › Format (the Format page). Once your Mac has its own page, it’s under Picture there.
  • If the sound runs ahead of or behind the picture, change Sound timing.
  • Headwire keeps what it learns about each capture card in its profile.

If you turned Camera off

Headwire shows Camera access is off.

  1. Tap Open Settings.
  2. In the Settings app, turn on Camera.
  3. Turn on Microphone too, for the sound.

Use your iPad as the Mac’s keyboard and mouse

You pair once from your Mac. From then on they connect by themselves.

A short video of this is on its way.
  1. Open Headwire.
  2. When Headwire asks, allow Bluetooth.
  3. On your Mac, open System Settings › Bluetooth.
  4. Under Nearby Devices, click Connect beside Headwire.
  5. Both screens show a code. Check that they match.
  6. On your Mac, confirm the code.
  7. On this iPad, tap Pair in the alert.

Your Mac lists this iPad under its own name, not as Headwire. Touch, a trackpad, a mouse and a keyboard on this iPad now reach the Mac: see Touch, pointer and keyboard.

Until this Mac can be controlled, Settings shows it as Capture card (Capture card in Settings).

Choose which device gets input

When a Bluetooth device first uses this iPad as its keyboard, Headwire asks whether to control it.

  • Tap Use It to control it.
  • Tap Not Now to leave it be.

To choose later:

  1. Open Settings › Add Mac….
  2. Tap Connect over Bluetooth….
  3. Under Devices, tap the one to control.

Pair Bluetooth without opening your Mac’s settings

When Headwire Connect is on your Mac and connected, it answers the Mac’s side of the Bluetooth pairing, so you compare one code on this iPad.

A short video of this is on its way.
  1. In Headwire, open Settings › Add Mac….
  2. Under Other ways, tap Connect over Bluetooth….
  3. Tap Pair. This iPad shows its own pairing alert.
  4. Check that the code in Headwire matches the one in the alert, then tap They match.
  5. Tap Pair in the alert. Headwire shows Paired.

If the codes differ, tap They don't match. Nothing is confirmed without your tap, and Headwire Connect gives up after a minute. If pairing doesn’t finish, Troubleshooting has every message.
