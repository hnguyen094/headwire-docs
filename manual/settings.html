Settings

Every screen in Settings, row by row: what each one does, and what Advanced mode adds.

Tap the settings button over the stream to open Settings. Each screen below is a row on its top level; tap Done to close the sheet.

Advanced mode. Settings starts short. Turn on Advanced mode in General to add the rows for connection choices, pairing keys, exact capture card formats and capture card details. It changes no setting by itself. A setting you changed stays in view with Advanced mode off, with Use Automatic beside it to go back.

At the top are your Macs, then a row for each screen.

A Mac’s page

A Mac’s page shows what the Mac on screen is doing and the settings that belong to that Mac alone. Some sections show only while the Mac is the one on screen.

A short video of this is on its way.
RowWhat it does
Show on screenWith more than one Mac paired, puts this Mac’s picture on screen. Shown while another Mac is on screen.
Picture and ControlsHow the picture arrives, by the capture card or a Headwire Connect connection, and how keys and clicks reach the Mac. With the capture card, Picture opens its format page in Advanced mode.
Picture, Connection and Controls choicesAdvanced mode. Choose where the picture comes from (Automatic, Capture card or Mac stream), which connection the Mac stream takes (Automatic, USB cable, Local network or Peer-to-peer Wi-Fi), and how controls reach the Mac (Automatic, Bluetooth or Headwire Connect). With Advanced mode off, a changed choice shows with Use Automatic.
Mac displayMirror main display shows the Mac’s own screen. Extend as a second display adds this screen to the Mac as another display; arrange it on your Mac in System Settings › Displays. Extending works when the picture comes from Headwire Connect.
Play sound onWhere this Mac’s sound plays. The first choice follows the setting in Sound; the others give this Mac its own.
Headwire ConnectWhether the Mac’s Headwire Connect is Connected or Not connected. In Advanced mode, Keys opens the pairing keys.
BluetoothWhether the Mac uses your iPad or iPhone as its Bluetooth keyboard and mouse. Connect over Bluetooth… gets ready for the Mac to connect; Stop waiting stops; Disconnect ends it. Bluetooth lets you type and point even with Headwire Connect closed.
Forget this MacRemoves the Mac and its pairing from your iPad or iPhone.

Keys

Advanced mode. Keys on a Mac’s page lists the key of the Mac’s Headwire Connect and the key of this iPad or iPhone. On your Mac, check that they match the keys in Headwire Connect’s Settings.

The capture card’s format

The capture card’s format page has the resolution and frame rate the capture card sends, and its profile. Headwire picks the best format by itself; choose one here only if you need to.

RowWhat it does
DeviceAdvanced mode, under Capture device: the capture card to use when more than one is plugged in.
Resolution and Frame rateUnder Format: what the capture card sends. Headwire marks the format it recommends. Use Automatic goes back to Headwire’s choice.
Pixel formatAdvanced mode, or while one is chosen: how the capture card packs colour. Automatic names the one in use.
Capture card profileOpens what Headwire knows about this capture card: its colour, modes and calibration. Capture card profiles covers it.

Picture

How the Mac’s picture fits this screen, and how Headwire Connect’s stream behaves.

A short video of this is on its way.
RowWhat it does
ScalingFit shows the whole picture, with black bars. Fill fills the screen and crops the edges. Zoom to content hides the black bars your Mac adds around its picture.
Optimize forWhat Headwire Connect’s stream favours: Automatic (chosen by what’s on screen), Responsiveness (frame rate first, for typing and games), Sharpness (full size first, for reading and design) or Battery Life (fewer frames). Battery Life is used while Low Power Mode is on.
HDROn a screen that shows HDR: Automatic switches to HDR while your Mac shows HDR content; Always stays in HDR, which uses more power on your Mac; Off.
Stream limitsAdvanced mode, or while set: the Largest size and Highest frame rate Headwire Connect streams at. It still adapts below them.
Sharpen pictureSharpens text when the picture is smaller than this screen. In Advanced mode it is Upscaling, with a choice of Off, MetalFX or Super Resolution where this iPad or iPhone has them.
Scaling when foldedOn a display that folds: the scaling used while the trackpad shares the screen.

Touch and pointer

How your fingers, a trackpad and a mouse move the Mac’s pointer. Touch, pointer and keyboard shows each in use.

A short video of this is on its way.
RowWhat it does
Touch your Mac directlyShown while Headwire Connect can take touches: your fingers tap, scroll and pinch on your Mac’s screen as on a touch screen.
TouchPoint and tap clicks where you tap. Trackpad moves the pointer as a trackpad does. Touch works in full screen; in a window, use Apple Pencil, a mouse or a trackpad.
Pointer speedHow fast the pointer moves.
Trackpad inertiaWith Trackpad: after a quick swipe, the pointer keeps moving and slows to a stop.
Pointer styleWith a Mac paired: draw the pointer as the Mac draws it, or as this iPad or iPhone does.
Snap to controlsWith this iPad’s or iPhone’s pointer style: with a mouse or trackpad, the pointer holds on to buttons on your Mac so clicks land on them, even a button inside another, such as a tab’s close button. A clickable area too large to highlight gets a soft tint instead.
ScribbleOn iPad, under Apple Pencil: write in a text field on your Mac to type.
Reverse scrollingFlips the scroll direction sent to the Mac, on top of the Mac’s own setting.
Three-finger dragThree fingers drag a window or a selection; Mission Control and Spaces then take four fingers. With Headwire Connect, it follows your Mac’s own setting, marked From Headwire Connect on your Mac.

Keyboard

Typing to your Mac: the on-screen keyboard, the keys iPadOS and iOS keep for themselves, and your own keys and shortcuts.

A short video of this is on its way.
RowWhat it does
AutocorrectionLets the on-screen keyboard correct words as you type to the Mac.
ShortcutsKeyboard shortcuts of your own for the stream. Touch, pointer and keyboard covers them.
Media and system keysAdvanced mode, or while changed: a row of media and system keys over the stream, Show, Auto-hide or Always hide.
Mac keysThe bar of Mac keys on the on-screen keyboard: Automatic, Show or Hide. On iPad, set it to Show to send the shortcuts iPadOS keeps for itself, such as Command-Tab.

Connections

Shown in Advanced mode, or while one of its settings is changed: a switch for each way the picture and the controls can reach your Mac, and Instant switch.

A short video of this is on its way.
RowWhat it does
Picture connectionsA switch for each way the picture can arrive: USB cable, Capture card, Local network (Ethernet), Local network (Wi-Fi) and Peer-to-peer Wi-Fi. A connection turned off is never used.
Control connectionsA switch for each way keys and clicks can reach the Mac: Bluetooth and Headwire Connect.
Instant switchKeeps a backup stream so a switch to another connection shows its next frame at once: Off, When charging, Smart (while charging, and on battery while the connection in use looks at risk) or Always. The footer says what it costs in battery. Smart is off in Low Power Mode.
Capture cardsOn a device that takes a capture card: every capture card you have plugged in, each with its profile. Capture card profiles covers them.
