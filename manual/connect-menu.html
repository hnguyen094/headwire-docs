The Headwire Connect menu

Headwire Connect lives in the menu bar on your Mac. Its menu has the two switches you use every day, your iPad and iPhone, and Pair a Device. Everything you set once is in its Settings window.

The menu

Click the Headwire Connect icon in the menu bar on your Mac. The menu is laid out like the Bluetooth menu: switches at the top, your devices in the middle, then Settings…, About Headwire Connect and Quit Headwire Connect.

A short video of this is on its way.

Streaming and Input

  • Streaming sends this Mac's screen and sound to your iPad or iPhone. It needs Screen & System Audio Recording in System Settings › Privacy & Security.
  • Input lets your iPad or iPhone control this Mac: the pointer, clicks, scrolling and typing. It needs Accessibility in System Settings › Privacy & Security.

Headwire Connect listens for your iPad or iPhone only while one of the two is on. With both off, Headwire says Streaming and Input are off and connects by itself once you turn one on.

Devices

Under Devices, each iPad or iPhone you've paired has a row with how it's connected: Not connected, or connected by USB cable, over Ethernet or over Wi-Fi. Point at a row to open its submenu, which says what it's doing with this Mac (showing this Mac, controlling it, or both), how it's connected, and offers:

  • Send a File, while it can take files, and a way to stop a file on its way.
  • Rename…, to give it a name of your own.
  • Forget, to remove the pairing. It has to pair again to connect.

While a device plays this Mac's sound, a line under the devices says so.

Pair a Device… is always at the end of the list. It opens the pairing window (see Pairing a device).

Capture Card

While a capture card is plugged into your Mac, the menu has a Capture Card submenu. Its second line names the display the capture card shows up as on your Mac, and its mode.

  • Display Mode sets the mode your Mac sends to the capture card. Match Headwire follows what Headwire on your iPad asks for; you can also pick the capture card's own mode, or 1080p at 50 Hz or 1080p at 30 Hz.
  • Capture Display picks which of the Mac's displays is the capture card. Automatic finds it by its name; choose it yourself when the menu says Choose the display your capture card is plugged into.

Match Headwire hears your iPad over Bluetooth. If Bluetooth is off or not allowed on your Mac, a line in the submenu says so.

When something needs fixing

Whatever stops Headwire Connect shows at the top of the menu, in plain words, with the button that fixes it. Fixes show whether or not Advanced mode is on.

  • A switch that needs a permission has a button under it, such as Allow Screen Recording… or Allow Accessibility…, which opens the right page of System Settings.
  • When macOS waits for you to allow Headwire Connect to open at login, the menu says Open at login needs your approval, with Open Login Items Settings….
  • If Headwire Connect can't read its key from your keychain, unlock your Mac and choose Try Again. If the key is damaged, choose Make New Key…, then pair each device again.
  • If your Mac couldn't switch the capture card to the mode asked for, the Capture Card submenu says so, with Try Again.

Advanced mode

Turn on Advanced mode in Headwire Connect's Settings › General. It adds rows in place and never moves or renames one:

  • which display is streamed, under Streaming, and whether each permission is allowed;
  • in each device's submenu, its key and when it paired;
  • in the Capture Card submenu, Restore previous mode on quit and the colour range Headwire asks for;
  • a line naming Headwire Connect's version.

Extend with Sidecar

Headwire Connect from the Headwire website has Extend with Sidecar, which lists the iPads near your Mac that can use Apple's own Sidecar, to connect or disconnect each. Headwire Connect from the App Store doesn't have it.

The Welcome window

The first time Headwire Connect opens, it shows Welcome to Headwire Connect: one line for Streaming and one for Input, each with Turn On, which turns it on and asks for its permission, then Open at login and pairing. Every permission is your choice; turn on only what you use. Choose Done when you're finished.

To see it again, open Headwire Connect's Settings › General and choose Show Welcome Window….

Pairing a device

Pairing makes sure your iPad or iPhone only ever talks to your Mac. You pair once; after that it connects by itself, by cable or wirelessly. A device on the USB cable can pair with no code at all (Pairing and more than one Mac has every way).

A short video of this is on its way.
  1. In the Headwire Connect menu on your Mac, choose Pair a Device…, then Show Pairing Code.
  2. On your iPad or iPhone, scan the code with the Camera app. Or, in Headwire, open Settings, choose Add Mac, then type the numbers.
  3. When it pairs, the window names your iPad or iPhone, and it appears under Devices. Choose Pair Another Device to pair the next one, or Done.

A code closes after five minutes; the window says when. If it expired, or the numbers were typed wrong too many times, the window says The pairing code expired or Pairing stopped: choose Show New Code.

Headwire Connect's Settings

Choose Settings… in the menu. The window has three tabs.

General

  • Open at login opens Headwire Connect when you log in to your Mac.
  • Permissions lists what the switches you've turned on need, each Allowed or with Allow….
  • Welcome window opens the first-run window again.
  • Advanced mode adds connection details, keys and capture card controls to the menu and Settings.

Headwire Connect from the Headwire website also has its update rows here.

Devices

  • Every paired iPad and iPhone, with how it's connected. Its info button shows its details; Control-click a row to Rename… it or Forget This Device….
  • Pair a Device… opens the pairing window.
  • Ask on this Mac before pairing: when it's off, an iPad or iPhone connected by USB cable or Bluetooth pairs without asking, even while this Mac is locked. Leave it off on a Mac with no screen.

Display

  • Let devices extend this Mac: an iPad or iPhone that chooses Extend gets a display of its own beside this Mac's. It needs Streaming on, and it's only in Headwire Connect from the Headwire website.
  • Capture Card: the same Display Mode and Capture Display as the menu, and, in Advanced mode, Restore previous mode on quit.
