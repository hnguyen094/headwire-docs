The Headwire Connect menu

What each part of the Headwire Connect menu on your Mac does, and its Welcome, pairing and Settings windows.

The menu

Click the Headwire Connect icon in the menu bar on your Mac. Like the Bluetooth menu, it has, from the top:

  • the Streaming and Input switches;
  • your devices, and Pair a Device…;
  • Extend with Sidecar and Capture Card, when they apply;
  • Settings…, About Headwire Connect and Quit Headwire Connect.
A short video of this is on its way.

Streaming and Input

  • Streaming sends this Mac’s screen and sound to your iPad or iPhone. It needs Screen & System Audio Recording in System Settings › Privacy & Security.
  • Input lets your iPad or iPhone control this Mac: the pointer, clicks, scrolling and typing. It needs Accessibility in System Settings › Privacy & Security.

Headwire Connect listens for your iPad or iPhone only while one of the two is on. With both off, Headwire says Streaming and Input are off, and connects by itself once you turn one on.

Devices

Under Devices, each iPad or iPhone you’ve paired says how it’s connected: Not connected, or by USB cable, over Ethernet or over Wi-Fi. Point at one to open its submenu, which says whether it shows this Mac, controls it, or both, and offers:

  • Send a File to name…, while it can take files, and a way to stop one on its way.
  • Rename…, to give it a name of your own.
  • Forget name…, to remove the pairing. It has to pair again to connect.

While a device plays this Mac’s sound, a line under the devices says so. Pair a Device…, at the end of the list, opens the pairing window.

Extend with Sidecar

Headwire Connect from the Headwire website has Extend with Sidecar, which lists the iPads near your Mac that can use Apple’s own Sidecar, to connect or disconnect each. Headwire Connect from the App Store doesn’t have it.

Capture Card

While a capture card is plugged into your Mac, the menu has a Capture Card submenu. Its second line names the display the capture card shows up as, and its mode.

  • Display Mode sets the mode your Mac sends to the capture card. Its four choices: Match Headwire, which follows what Headwire on your iPad asks for; the capture card’s own mode; 1080p at 50 Hz; and 1080p at 30 Hz.
  • Capture Display picks which of the Mac’s displays is the capture card. Automatic finds it by its name; choose it yourself when the menu says Choose the display your capture card is plugged into.

Match Headwire hears your iPad over Bluetooth. If Bluetooth is off or not allowed on your Mac, a line in the submenu says so.

When something needs fixing

Whatever stops Headwire Connect shows at the top of the menu, in plain words, with the button that fixes it, whether or not Advanced mode is on.

  • A missing permission: the switch that needs it has a button under it that opens the right page of System Settings.
  • Open at login waiting: the menu says Open at login needs your approval, with Open Login Items Settings….
  • A key it can’t read: unlock your Mac and choose Try Again. If the key is damaged, choose Make New Key…, then pair each device again.
  • A capture card mode that didn’t take: the Capture Card submenu says so, with Try Again.

Advanced mode

Turn on Advanced mode in Headwire Connect’s Settings › General. It adds rows in place and never moves or renames one:

  • which display is streamed, under Streaming, and whether each permission is allowed;
  • in each device’s submenu, its key and when it paired;
  • in the Capture Card submenu, Restore previous mode on quit and the colour range Headwire asks for;
  • a line naming Headwire Connect’s version.

The Welcome window

The Welcome window opens the first time Headwire Connect does: a line for Streaming and one for Input, each with Turn On, then Open at login and pairing. Turn on only what you use. Set up Headwire Connect and see your Mac walks through it. To see it again, choose Show Welcome Window… in Settings › General.

The pairing window

Pair a Device… opens the pairing window with a code to scan or type. Pairing and more than one Mac has every way to pair.

A short video of this is on its way.
  • When a device pairs, the window names it and it appears under Devices. Choose Pair Another Device for the next one, or Done.
  • A code closes after five minutes; the window says when.
  • If the window says The pairing code expired or Pairing stopped, choose Show New Code.

Headwire Connect’s Settings

Choose Settings… in the menu. The window has three tabs.

A short video of this is on its way.

General

  • Open at login opens Headwire Connect when you log in to your Mac.
  • Permissions lists what the switches you’ve turned on need, each Allowed or with Allow….
  • Welcome window has Show Welcome Window….
  • Advanced mode adds connection details, keys and capture card controls to the menu and Settings.

Headwire Connect from the Headwire website also has its update rows here.

Devices

  • Every paired iPad and iPhone, with how it’s connected. Its info button shows its details; Control-click a row to Rename… it or Forget This Device….
  • Pair a Device… opens the pairing window.
  • Ask on this Mac before pairing: when it’s off, an iPad or iPhone connected by USB cable or Bluetooth pairs without asking, even while this Mac is locked. Leave it off on a Mac with no screen.
  • This Mac's key, in Advanced mode: the key to compare with Keys on this iPad or iPhone. Each device’s own key is in its submenu in Advanced mode.

Display

  • Let devices extend this Mac: an iPad or iPhone that chooses Extend gets a display of its own beside this Mac’s. It needs Streaming on, and it’s only in Headwire Connect from the Headwire website.
  • Capture Card: the same Display Mode and Capture Display as the menu, and, in Advanced mode, Restore previous mode on quit.
