The stream and its controls

Headwire has one screen: your Mac, edge to edge. Everything else sits in a few glass buttons over it, and folds away while you use the Mac.

The screen

Once Headwire has a picture of your Mac, from a capture card or from Headwire Connect, it fills the screen. Touches, a trackpad, a mouse and a keyboard all go to the Mac (see Touch, pointer and keyboard).

A short video of this is on its way.

How the picture fits

In Settings › Picture, Scaling sets how the Mac's screen fits this iPad or iPhone:

  • Fit shows the whole of the Mac's screen, with black bars where the shapes differ. It's where Headwire starts.
  • Fill fills the screen and trims the Mac's edges.
  • Zoom to content fills the screen with the Mac's own picture, cropping off any black border around it.

Keeping the screen on

While your Mac's picture shows, Headwire keeps this iPad or iPhone from locking. Turn off Keep screen awake in Settings › General to let it lock as usual. When the Mac sends no picture, because it's asleep, the screen dims and locks as it would without Headwire.

The buttons over the stream

The buttons sit in a row at the top corner. While your Mac's picture shows, they fold into a chevron so they keep out of the way.

A short video of this is on its way.
  • Tap the chevron to open the row, and tap it again to fold it. The row also folds when you use one of its buttons, or by itself after a few seconds.
  • While you use the Mac, the row is put away and the chevron dims. Tap it, swipe down from the top edge, or move the pointer to the top to bring the row back.
  • With Hide controls completely on in Settings › General, even the chevron goes while you use the Mac. Swipe down from the top to bring the controls back.
  • With no picture, the row stays open. With VoiceOver or Switch Control on, the chevron stays in sight at full strength.

The row holds, from the leading end:

ButtonWhat it does
CaptureScreenshots, recordings and Live Text of your Mac (below).

The key row

iPadOS keeps some keys from apps: back and forward, Mission Control, the apps list, brightness and the media keys. The key row puts them on screen as glass buttons along the bottom. It's hidden until you choose otherwise in Settings › Keyboard › Media and system keys:

  • Show keeps the row over the picture.
  • Auto-hide shows it when you touch the screen and fades it with the settings button when you stop.
  • Always hide leaves it off.

A key the connection in use can't send is drawn dimmed.

What the stream tells you

When there's no picture to show, the screen says why, and what to do next.

A short video of this is on its way.
  • No video: nothing gives Headwire a picture yet. Connection options shows every way to connect and what each needs.
  • Starting capture… or Connecting to your Mac…: the picture is on its way.
  • Your Mac is asleep or not sending a picture: the capture card runs, but the Mac sends nothing. Wake your Mac or check its HDMI cable; the picture comes back by itself.
  • Your Mac's display is asleep: Headwire Connect is connected, but the Mac's display sleeps. Wake your Mac to see its screen.
  • Capture paused: the system paused the capture card, for example while another app uses it or Headwire isn't the only app on screen. It says which, and capture resumes by itself.
  • Reconnecting…: the last picture stays up, dimmed, while Headwire finds its way back to the Mac.

Anything else the stream says, such as a Mac that can't stream yet, is in Troubleshooting, in the same words.

Notes over the picture

A few notes show at the top for a moment without covering the picture:

  • This iPad is warm (or This iPhone is warm): upscaling and Instant switch are off until it cools down, so capture keeps running.
  • Protected video can't be shown: streaming apps black out their video on capture cards. The rest of your screen still works.
  • Capture is slowing down: the capture card is sending fewer frames. Unplug it for a few minutes, then plug it back in.

Screenshots, recordings and Live Text

The Capture button takes pictures of your Mac's screen as Headwire shows it, with nothing to install or set up on the Mac. It works while there's a picture.

A short video of this is on its way.
  • Take screenshot opens the picture full screen. Choose Markup to draw on it with Apple Pencil or a finger, or Live Text to select, copy, translate and look up its text. Then Share it, Save to Photos, or tap Done.
  • Record screen records the Mac's picture. A red dot with the time recorded stays in sight while it runs; tap it, or choose Stop recording, to stop. The clip then opens to Share or Save to Photos.
  • Look at text takes a screenshot and opens it ready for Live Text.

With a keyboard, Control-Option-Command-S takes a screenshot, Control-Option-Command-R starts or stops recording, and Control-Option-Command-T looks at text.

Clipboard and files

With Headwire Connect, text you copy on your Mac is ready to paste on this iPad or iPhone, and files move both ways.

A short video of this is on its way.
  • Copy text on your Mac, and paste it in any app here. Copies stay on this iPad or iPhone, and don't reach your other devices.
  • To paste text from here on your Mac, press Command-V on a keyboard with the stream in front, or use Paste to Mac on the Mac keys over the on-screen keyboard. Headwire only reads your clipboard when you paste.
  • Drop text on the stream to type it where it lands.
  • Drop files or images on the stream to send them to the Downloads folder on your Mac.
  • Files your Mac sends land in Files, in the Headwire folder.

Each transfer shows over the stream while it runs, and says where it went once it ends.

Sound

Your Mac's sound plays from whichever picture is on screen: the capture card's HDMI sound, or the sound Headwire Connect streams. When the picture moves from one to the other, the sound moves with it.

A short video of this is on its way.

For a capture card, Headwire needs Microphone access, because the system treats the capture card's sound as a microphone. Headwire only takes the capture card's sound, never the built-in microphone. Where the sound plays, its volume and its timing are in Settings › Sound.

iPhone Duo and a fold

On iPhone Duo, open flat or closed, the stream fills the screen as it does on any iPhone. Half folded, the stream takes the upper or leading half and a trackpad for your Mac takes the other.

A short video of this is on its way.
  • Held like a laptop, the lower half is a keyboard deck: the key row along its top, the Mac keys under it, and the trackpad below. The keyboard key brings up the on-screen keyboard over the trackpad, and the picture stays put.
  • The trackpad half drives the Mac even with no picture, while you watch the Mac's own display.
  • Folded, the picture uses its own Scaling when folded in Settings › Picture, Fit to start with. The setting shows once this iPhone has been folded.
