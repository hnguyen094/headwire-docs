Headwire Connect

Set up Headwire Connect, the Mac app for Headwire, to see and control your Mac from your iPad or iPhone over a USB cable or wirelessly, with the most features Headwire has.

It is one of two ways to use Headwire. The other, a capture card and Bluetooth, needs nothing installed on your Mac. Use either, or both: Headwire picks the connection and moves to another when one drops.

What you need

A short video of this is on its way.
  • A Mac with Apple silicon and macOS 27 or later.
  • Headwire Connect on that Mac, from Get Headwire Connect.
  • A USB cable between your Mac and your iPad or iPhone, or both on the same network.

Headwire Connect comes in two builds:

  • Headwire Connect from the Headwire website adds what Apple doesn’t allow in the Mac App Store, such as extending your Mac onto your iPad or iPhone as a second display.
  • Headwire Connect from the App Store needs a display connected to the Mac to stream, such as a monitor, the capture card or an HDMI dummy plug.

Set up Headwire Connect and see your Mac

The first time it opens, Headwire Connect shows the Welcome to Headwire Connect window. Its icon stays in the menu bar, where the Headwire Connect menu is.

A short video of this is on its way.
  1. On your Mac, in the Welcome window, click Turn On beside Streaming, then allow Headwire Connect in Screen & System Audio Recording.
  2. Click Turn On beside Input, then allow Headwire Connect in Accessibility.
  3. In Headwire, open Settings › Add Mac….
  4. Under Macs nearby, tap your Mac. If it isn’t there, see Your Mac isn’t in Add Mac.
  5. If Headwire asks for a code, on your Mac, click Show Pairing Code in the Welcome window.
  6. In Headwire, type the 12 numbers your Mac shows. Headwire pairs once the last one is in, and your Mac’s screen appears.
  • Over a USB cable there’s no code to type. Every other way to pair, and pairing more than one Mac, is in Pairing and more than one Mac.
  • Turn on Open at login in the Welcome window so Headwire Connect is there whenever your Mac starts.
  • To see the Welcome window again, choose Settings… in the Headwire Connect menu, then Show Welcome Window….

Headwire Connect listens for your iPad or iPhone only while Streaming or Input is on. With both off, Headwire can’t find your Mac.

How your iPad or iPhone connects

Once paired, Headwire connects to your Mac by itself whenever it can reach it, in whichever of three ways works best:

A short video of this is on its way.
ConnectionWhat it needs
USB cableA USB cable between your Mac and your iPad or iPhone. It has the shortest delay, and it charges your iPad or iPhone.
Local networkYour Mac and your iPad or iPhone on the same network, over Wi-Fi or over Ethernet on a USB-C dock or adapter. Headwire prefers Ethernet when both reach your Mac.
Peer-to-peer Wi-FiNo network at all: your iPad or iPhone talks to your Mac’s Wi-Fi directly. It’s slower than a local network, so Headwire uses it only when there is no shared network.

The picture and the controls are chosen separately. On iPad, the picture can come from the capture card or from Headwire Connect, and the controls can go over Bluetooth or through Headwire Connect, in any mix. Headwire moves to another connection when one drops, without a blank frame and without leaving a key held down on your Mac.

  • To keep Headwire on one connection, change Picture, Connection or Controls from Automatic on a Mac’s page.
  • To turn a connection off, or keep a backup stream ready with Instant switch, use Connections.

Allow Local Network access

The first time Headwire looks for your Mac, iOS asks whether Headwire may find devices on your local network. Choose Allow to connect over a local network or peer-to-peer Wi-Fi. A USB cable, a capture card and Bluetooth work without it.

A short video of this is on its way.

If you chose Don’t Allow, Add Mac says Local Network access is off.

  1. Tap Open Settings.
  2. In the Settings app, turn on Local Network.
