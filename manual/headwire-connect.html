Headwire Connect

Headwire Connect is the Mac app for Headwire. With it, your iPad or iPhone shows your Mac’s screen and plays its sound over a USB cable or wirelessly, and controls the Mac with the most features Headwire has.

It is one of two ways to use Headwire. The other, a capture card and Bluetooth, needs nothing installed on your Mac. Use either, or both: Headwire picks the connection and moves to another when one drops.

Get Headwire Connect

Install Headwire Connect on the Mac you want to see, not on your iPad or iPhone. It needs a Mac with Apple silicon and macOS 27 or later. There are two builds:

A short video of this is on its way.
  • Headwire Connect from the Headwire website adds what Apple doesn’t allow in the Mac App Store, such as letting your iPad or iPhone extend your Mac as a second display. Headwire says when a feature needs it.
  • Headwire Connect from the App Store needs a display connected to the Mac to stream, such as a monitor, the capture card or an HDMI dummy plug.

Get Headwire Connect has the download and the steps to install it.

The first time on your Mac

When Headwire Connect opens for the first time, it shows the Welcome to Headwire Connect window. Its icon stays in the menu bar at the top of the screen; the Headwire Connect menu is there.

A short video of this is on its way.
  1. Turn on Streaming to show your Mac’s screen and play its sound on your iPad or iPhone. macOS asks to allow Headwire Connect in Screen & System Audio Recording; allow it.
  2. Turn on Input to control your Mac from your iPad or iPhone. macOS asks to allow Headwire Connect in Accessibility; allow it.
  3. Turn on Open at login so Headwire Connect is there whenever your Mac starts.
  4. Under Pair your iPad or iPhone, click Show Pairing Code, then pair as Pairing and more than one Mac describes.
  5. Click Done.

Headwire Connect listens for your iPad or iPhone only while Streaming or Input is on. With both off, Headwire can’t find your Mac, and the Welcome window says “Turn on Streaming or Input to pair a device”.

To see the window again, on your Mac, choose Settings… in the Headwire Connect menu, then Show Welcome Window… under General.

How your iPad or iPhone connects

Once paired, Headwire connects to your Mac by itself whenever it can reach it. It reaches Headwire Connect in three ways, and uses whichever works best:

A short video of this is on its way.
ConnectionWhat it needs
USB cableA USB cable between your Mac and your iPad or iPhone. It has the shortest delay, and it charges your iPad or iPhone.
Local networkYour Mac and your iPad or iPhone on the same network, over Wi-Fi or over Ethernet on a USB-C dock or adapter. Headwire prefers Ethernet when both reach your Mac.
Peer-to-peer Wi-FiNo network at all: your iPad or iPhone talks to your Mac’s Wi-Fi directly. It’s slower than a local network, so Headwire uses it only when there is no shared network.

The picture and the controls are chosen separately. On iPad, the picture can come from the capture card or from Headwire Connect, and the controls can go over Bluetooth or through Headwire Connect, in any mix. Headwire moves to another connection when one drops, without a blank frame and without leaving a key held down on your Mac.

To choose a connection yourself, open a Mac’s page in Settings: its Picture, Connection and Controls choices each start at Automatic. A choice other than Automatic keeps Headwire on that connection, and Headwire says why when it can’t use it. Turning a connection off altogether, and Instant switch, which keeps a backup stream ready, are on the Connections screen.

Local Network permission

The first time Headwire looks for your Mac, iOS asks whether Headwire may find devices on your local network. Choose Allow to connect over a local network or peer-to-peer Wi-Fi. A USB cable, a capture card and Bluetooth work without it.

A short video of this is on its way.

If you chose Don’t Allow, Add Mac says Local Network access is off, with Open Settings to turn it on: in the Settings app on your iPad or iPhone, open Apps › Headwire and turn on Local Network.
