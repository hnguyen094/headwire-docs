Capture card profiles

Headwire keeps a profile for each capture card you plug in: what it has learnt about the capture card’s picture, the format you chose for it, and its sound timing. It uses the profile to correct the picture by itself, and you can look inside or change it.

What a profile holds

Each capture card gets its own profile the first time this iPad sees it, and keeps it when you plug it in again. Two capture cards of the same model each have their own.

A short video of this is on its way.
  • Status and USB ID: whether the capture card is connected, and which one it is.
  • Brightness range: whether the capture card’s blacks and whites arrive as labelled. Many capture cards label their range wrongly, which makes blacks look grey or crushed; Headwire notices and corrects it.
  • Colour: which colour matrix the capture card uses, so reds stay red.
  • Mode: the capture card’s own mode, and how many frames it really delivers.
  • Calibration: what the last calibration measured.
  • Sound: the capture card’s sound timing.
  • Measurements: what Headwire measured, kept for each Mac and each display mode, so a capture card used with two Macs keeps what it learnt about each.
  • Known behaviour: what the capture card has been seen to do, such as reconnecting, or slowing down over long sessions.

Open a capture card’s profile

Profiles are in Advanced mode.

A short video of this is on its way.
  1. Turn on Advanced mode in Settings › General.
  2. Open Settings › Connections. Every capture card is listed under Capture cards, connected or last seen.
  3. Tap the capture card. Its profile opens.

The capture card plugged in now also has Capture card profile on its Format page, opened from Format on the Capture card page, or Picture on the Mac’s page.

Correct the picture yourself

Headwire picks the range and colour on its own. Change them only if the picture looks wrong.

A short video of this is on its way.
  • Under Brightness range, Read as offers Automatic, Video (16–235) and Full (0–255). Change it only if blacks look grey or crushed. Detected says what Headwire found.
  • Under Colour, Read as offers Automatic, HD (BT.709) and SD (BT.601). Change it only if colours look off, such as reds turning orange.

Calibration

With Headwire Connect on your Mac, Headwire measures each capture card with a small test pattern. Headwire Connect shows a narrow strip in the corner of the Mac’s screen for under a second, and Headwire reads it from the capture card’s own frames.

A short video of this is on its way.
  • It runs by itself the first time a capture card, one of its modes, or a mode your Mac sends is seen, while Headwire Connect is connected and the capture card is streaming.
  • It measures the brightness range and colour the capture card really sends, how much fine detail survives, the time from your Mac to this iPad, and the frames the capture card delivers.
  • Calibrate now runs it again. Turn off Calibrate automatically to stop the automatic runs for that capture card. A capture card has to be streaming to calibrate.
  • Last calibration shows what it found.

Without Headwire Connect, Headwire still learns the range from the picture while it streams; it just can’t measure the rest.

Notes over the picture

Some of what Headwire learns shows for a few seconds over the stream, such as Protected video can't be shown and Capture is slowing down. Notes over the picture says what each means.

A short video of this is on its way.

Forget a capture card

Forget capture card, at the bottom of its profile, removes the profile and the format you chose for it. Plug the capture card in again to start a new one.
