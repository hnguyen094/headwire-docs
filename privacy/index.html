Headwire Privacy Policy

Last updated 2026-10-23

Headwire collects nothing about you unless you turn on Share usage data. There are no accounts, no advertising, no tracking and no Headwire servers. Your Mac’s screen, its sound, your input and anything you copy or send stay on your devices, or travel directly between your own iPad or iPhone and your own Mac. The only things the app ever sends over the internet are anonymous usage data and crash reports, which are off until you turn them on (see Usage data).

What this covers

This policy covers Headwire for iPad and iPhone, and Headwire Helper for Mac, both the App Store version and the Direct download. Hung Nguyen develops and publishes both apps. “We” isn’t used here: there is no company and no team behind the apps that could see your data.

What the developer collects

Nothing, unless you choose to send it:

  • Usage data and crash reports, from Headwire on iPad and iPhone, only if you turn on Share usage data (see Usage data).
  • Feedback you email with Send feedback (see Feedback).

Headwire Helper sends nothing to the developer or to anyone else. Neither app contains an advertising library, and neither connects to a server of the developer’s own. The PostHog library in Headwire for iPad and iPhone includes a crash reporter, which Headwire turns on only while Share usage data is on, so no crash report is sent unless you’ve turned it on. Apart from these, the only reports the developer can see are the ones Apple passes on, and only if you’ve chosen to share them (see What Apple handles).

Usage data

Share usage data is off until you turn it on. The first time you open Headwire, it asks once whether to turn it on, with Share and Not now; after that it’s in Settings › About › Share usage data. While it’s on, Headwire for iPad and iPhone sends anonymous usage data and crash reports to PostHog, a product analytics service, so the developer can see which features are used and fix what breaks.

  • What is sent: the name of each thing you do in the app, such as opening Settings or turning natural scrolling on, and once a minute a count of frequent ones, such as touches and key presses. With each one, PostHog’s software adds the app’s version, the iOS or iPadOS version, the device model and type (such as iPad), its screen size, its language and region setting, its time zone, whether it’s on Wi-Fi or mobile data, and a random ID made on this install.
  • Crash reports: when Headwire crashes, the next launch sends what kind of crash it was, the addresses in Headwire’s and the system’s code where it happened, the names of those code files and their build IDs, with the same app and device details. A crash’s message is taken out before it’s sent, since it could quote something on screen, and so are full file paths.
  • What is never sent: your Mac’s screen or sound, anything you type, paste or copy, file names, the names of your Macs or devices, pairing codes, your name, Apple Account or email address, your location, or an advertising identifier. Headwire doesn’t identify you to PostHog or build a profile of you, and the data isn’t used for tracking or advertising, sold, or shared with anyone else.
  • Where it goes: PostHog’s cloud in the European Union (Frankfurt, Germany). Like any server, PostHog sees the device’s IP address when the data arrives; Headwire asks it not to work out a location from it, and the project is set to discard it rather than store it.
  • How long it’s kept: up to one year, after which PostHog deletes it.
  • How to turn it off: turn off Share usage data. Sending stops at once, and the random ID and anything not yet sent are deleted from the device; turning it on again starts with a new ID. Deleting Headwire does the same. Because what was sent isn’t linked to you, it can’t be picked out and deleted afterwards; it is removed when the year is up.
  • Headwire never sends usage data over a network you’ve set to Low Data Mode.

Feedback

Settings › About › Send feedback opens your own email app with a message to the developer, with the app and system versions filled in. Nothing is sent until you send it yourself. What you write, with your email address, reaches the developer’s mailbox (Gmail), and is used only to read and answer your feedback.

What stays on your devices

To work, the apps keep a few things on the device they run on. None of it leaves that device except inside your own device backups.

  • Settings: your choices in Settings, the connections you’ve turned on or off, and your input and sound preferences.
  • Capture card profiles: the card’s model and the adjustments Headwire made for it, so the picture is right the next time you plug it in.
  • Pairing keys: each app makes its own key and keeps it, with the keys of the devices it’s paired with and their names, in the Keychain, readable only on that device. They aren’t synced through iCloud Keychain and don’t move to a new device.
  • Your Mac’s keyboard layout and shortcuts, as Headwire Helper last reported them, so Bluetooth input types the right characters when the helper isn’t running.
  • Custom keys: the keys, text snippets and key sequences you make, for each Mac and for each app on the Mac.
  • Your Macs: which paired Mac you chose to show, which one the capture card is plugged into, and the connection each was last shown over.
  • Your paired Macs’ names, and whether each is connected: shared with Headwire’s widgets and controls, Siri, Shortcuts and Spotlight on this device, so you can open a Mac from them. Pairing keys are never shared with them.
  • Battery readings: a reading of the device’s charge each minute, kept for two days, to work out what Instant switch costs in battery.
  • How many times you’ve opened Headwire, so it knows when to offer a tip.
  • Files you receive from your Mac, in Headwire’s folder in the Files app (On My iPad › Headwire), until you delete them. On the Mac, files you send from the iPad go to your Downloads folder.
  • Logs. Both apps write diagnostic logs to the system log on the device they run on. They name what happened, not what you did: no keys you press, text you type, pairing codes or keys, and device and Mac names are replaced with a scrambled value. Logs stay on the device unless you collect and share them yourself.

Deleting Headwire removes its settings and files from that device. On the Mac, removing a device from Headwire Helper’s menu forgets its key.

What travels between your devices

Headwire connects your iPad or iPhone to a Mac in the same room. What it sends goes straight between those two devices:

  • From a capture card: the Mac’s picture and sound come into the iPad over the card’s USB cable and are shown and played there. They never leave the iPad.
  • To the Mac over Bluetooth: the iPad acts as a Bluetooth keyboard and mouse and sends your touches, pointer movements and key presses to the Mac you paired it with.
  • Through Headwire Helper, over a USB cable, your local network (Ethernet or Wi-Fi) or a direct wireless link between the two devices:
    • the Mac’s screen and sound, to the iPad or iPhone;
    • your touches, pointer movements and key presses, to the Mac;
    • plain text you copy, both ways (see Copy and paste);
    • files you choose to send, both ways;
    • the Mac’s keyboard layout and shortcuts, and settings such as the picture size and frame rate the device asks for;
    • the name and identifier of the app in front on the Mac, when the device connects and each time you switch apps, so the device can show that app’s custom keys;
    • whether the Mac’s screen is on, asleep or locked, whether it’s streaming and whether the capture card is on it;
    • small pictures of the Mac’s screen, at most one a second, only while the Mac overview is open on a paired device. They’re shown there and not saved.

How the helper link is protected

  • Every connection between Headwire and Headwire Helper is encrypted with TLS 1.3, and each side checks the other’s key against the one it saved when you paired them. A device that hasn’t been paired gets nothing from the helper.
  • Headwire Helper only accepts connections from this Mac, its USB cable, its local networks and direct wireless. It refuses connections that arrive through a VPN or from the internet, and it never opens a port for the internet.
  • Headwire never uses mobile data for the link.
  • Headwire Helper captures the screen only while a paired device is connected.
  • On your local network, Headwire Helper announces itself with Bonjour under your Mac’s name, as Mac file and screen sharing do, with a random tag that only your paired devices can recognise.

Copy and paste

  • While a device is connected, text you copy on the Mac is sent to it and put on its clipboard. Text marked as private, such as a password copied from a password manager, is never sent.
  • Headwire reads the iPad’s or iPhone’s clipboard only when you paste into the Mac, with ⌘V on the stream, Paste to Mac in Settings, or the Paste clipboard to Mac action in Shortcuts or Siri. It never reads it on its own.
  • Clipboard contents are never logged, and Headwire keeps no copy of them.

Permissions, and why each is asked for

Each permission is asked for when you first use what needs it. You can turn any of them off in Settings on the iPad or iPhone, or in System Settings › Privacy & Security on the Mac; only the feature that needs it stops working.

The App Store version of Headwire Helper runs in Apple’s app sandbox, and can only reach your Downloads folder and the files you pick in an Open dialog.

Live Text on a screenshot, and the screenshots and recordings themselves, are made on the device. Nothing is sent anywhere to read text or to process a picture.

Picture in picture draws the Mac’s screen on the device itself. For it, Headwire keeps playing while it’s in the background, as a video app does; nothing else changes about what it sends.

What Apple handles

  • Purchases. Tips and Headwire Pro are bought through the App Store. Apple handles the payment and your Apple Account; Headwire only learns from the App Store which products you own, and checks that on the device to unlock Pro and count your tips. The developer gets sales reports from Apple that don’t name you.
  • Crash reports and diagnostics. If you’ve turned on sharing with app developers in your device’s Analytics & Improvements settings, Apple may pass crash reports and diagnostics from Headwire to the developer, without your name. That’s your choice in Apple’s settings, and off unless you turn it on.
  • Siri. When you speak a request to Siri, such as opening a Mac by its name, Apple processes the request, the Mac’s name included. Headwire itself sends nothing.

Apple’s handling of this data is covered by Apple’s privacy policy.

Links to other websites

Headwire opens some web pages in your browser, such as this policy and Apple’s Terms of Use. Those websites have their own privacy policies. This page is hosted by GitHub Pages, which may keep logs of visits under GitHub’s privacy statement; the apps send nothing to it.

Children

Headwire isn’t directed at children. It collects nothing from anyone, children included, unless they turn on Share usage data or send feedback.

Changes to this policy

If what the apps keep or send ever changes, this page changes first, with a new date at the top. The page’s history is public in the headwire-docs repository.

Contact

For a question about privacy in Headwire, open an issue at github.com/hnguyen094/headwire-docs/issues. Issues there are public, so leave out anything personal.
